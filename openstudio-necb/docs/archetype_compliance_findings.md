# Do the legacy NECB 2020 archetypes meet the NECB 2020 performance path?

**Finding: broadly, no.** Run through an independently verified NECB 2020/2025
reference-building pipeline, 15 of the 17 testable legacy archetypes fail the
full 8.4.1.2 determination at Toronto/Electricity; only FullServiceRestaurant
and LargeHotel pass. Switching the fleet to NaturalGas widens the energy gap
by 1–11 points. This document states the method, the numbers, the failure
drivers, and the caveats, so the finding can be assessed — and challenged —
on evidence.

*Prepared 2026-08-03 by Claude (Anthropic's AI assistant) under phylroy's
direction, from the openstudio-necb pipeline on branch `phylroy_dnd`. Every
number traces to a run artifact; decision IDs (D-xx) reference
`necb_decisions.md`.*

## Method

- **The proposed building is the legacy archetype itself**, generated by
  `openstudio-standards` `model_create_prototype_model(template: 'NECB2020')`
  — geometry, loads, schedules, and HVAC exactly as the legacy toolchain
  ships them.
- **The reference building is generated by the openstudio-necb pipeline**, a
  seven-gem SDK-only implementation of NECB Part 8 whose reference machinery
  has been verified against the printed code: the Table 8.4.4.7.-A/-B
  selection row-by-row (D-58: all 97 buildable proposed systems, two naming
  passes, adjudicated golden), every equipment-efficiency minimum against
  Tables 5.2.12.1.-A..-P (D-59/D-60: exact, both editions), and the vendored
  data re-audited after the code-extraction service's fixes (D-61: zero
  stale values).
- **The determination is the full 8.4.1.2**: annual energy (sentence (2)),
  unmet heating ≤ 100 h (sentence (3)), unmet cooling within the allowance
  over the reference (sentence (4)), with the sentence-(5) capacity
  increase applied per thermal block (secant-targeted, bounded, stall
  detection — D-43).
- Primary axis: Toronto CWEC2020, Electricity, true 8760 h annual runs.
  Secondary axes as noted (NaturalGas week fleet; 2025 edition; Edmonton /
  Yellowknife spot runs).

NaturalGas (week mode, 2020): every building reads 1–11 points *stricter*

## Why they fail — three distinct drivers

1. **Energy, over target (most buildings, 105–118%).** The reference the
   code prescribes is simply better than the archetypes: allowance-level
   lighting with the photocontrols 4.2.2 mandates (D-51/D-57), code-minimum
   envelope, right-sized staged equipment with economizers (D-46/D-62),
   energy recovery wherever 5.2.10.1 requires it, and water-side economizers
   on fan-coil systems (D-56).
2. **Unmet-cooling hours (the residential fleet).** The archetypes' own
   PTAC-cooled blocks leave 126–2 752 h of occupied cooling unmet annually.
   Against the code-required *identical-copy* reference (Table -A residential
   rule, D-58) — which overheats less because it carries allowance-level
   lighting — sentence (4) fails even after capacity iteration. The old
   through-the-wall reference masked exactly this (its own unmet hours made
   the +10% test easy), which is why the apartments read ~100% before the
   selection defect was fixed.
3. **Iteration-resistant micro-zones (documented characteristics).**
   SmallHotel's reference fails sentence (3) on four < 5 m² storage closets
   whose autosized baseboards lag morning setback recovery ~0.5–0.9 h every
   day (D-64); the legacy proposed hides identical physics behind hard-sized
   equipment (L-23).

## Cross-checks that constrain the finding

- The two editions produce **kWh-identical references** on every comparable
  building; every 2020↔2025 difference in the table is the sentence-(4)
  allowance wording acting on the capacity iteration — nowhere else (D-60
  sweep amendments).
- The pipeline's fixed-point premise was validated in the other direction:
  where the archetypes and the code agree (FSR, LargeHotel), the
  determination passes.
- The selection matrix, efficiency tables, daylighting control matrix, and
  ERV/pump/envelope/exterior data were each verified against the printed
  code independently of the legacy implementation (D-58…D-61).

## Caveats, honestly stated

- Toronto/Electricity is the only axis with full-annual runs fleet-wide;
  NaturalGas is week-mode; Edmonton/Yellowknife are spot runs.
- Two archetypes cannot be generated by the legacy toolchain at all
  (NorthernEducation/NorthernHealthCare, L-27) and three LEEP models carry
  an unresolvable `- undefined -` space function (L-28) — the finding covers
  the 19 buildable, taggable archetypes.
- The capacity iteration inflates failing proposeds' energy (by design —
  sentence (5) makes them meet their loads first); percentages for
  unmet-heavy buildings (Hospital, Outpatient, apartments) are therefore
  upper bounds tied to the iteration's convergence, and one loaded-machine
  reproducibility flutter is on record (D-62 notes): rerun solo before
  attributing single-run movement.
- Legacy performs no full-annual 8.4.1.2 determination of its own (its
  `performance_compliance` module is absent — see the companion issue), so
  there is no second implementation to arbitrate; the printed-code
  verifications above are the substitute.

## What this means practically

The archetypes remain excellent *typical* buildings; the finding is that
they are not *code-minimum* buildings under NECB 2020's own performance
path. Anyone using them as compliance baselines, or as stand-ins for
"NECB 2020-compliant stock", inherits a 1–18% optimism depending on
building and fuel.
